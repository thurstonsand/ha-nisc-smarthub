# Cobb EMC bill anatomy

Every line a residential NiteFlex bill can carry, for a service location inside the City of Roswell, Fulton County, Georgia, and how each one is computed. Sources are Cobb EMC's own rate schedules and member pages, its board-approved Service Rules and Regulations, the Georgia Code, and the Georgia Department of Revenue rate charts. Read 2026-09-11. The account has no posted bill yet (see [smarthub-api.md](smarthub-api.md)), so anything marked bill-only below is still unobserved for this location.

The tariff facts themselves are transcribed in [niteflex-tariff.md](niteflex-tariff.md); this file confirms them and covers everything that sits around them.

## Energy, service charge, proration

The schedule's rates and periods are as transcribed already, and the PDF confirms them at the 2026-01-01 effective date. Two clauses matter beyond the numbers. The minimum charge is $33.00 plus the PCA, which only binds if a cycle's energy is free, so on NiteFlex it can bind only when every kWh lands in the first 400 Super Off-Peak kWh. And the billing period clause says readings come at intervals of approximately thirty days and that "the charges set forth in the Monthly Rate may be prorated to reflect a thirty-day billing period."

That proration language is the loose thread. It says "may," it does not give a formula, and it does not say whether the 400 kWh allowance prorates along with the service charge. Cobb EMC applies it to seasonal splits on the Standard rate, where a cycle straddles the May and October boundaries; NiteFlex has no seasonal rates, so the only candidate here is a short or long cycle, and the first bill is exactly that case since service connected on 2026-08-28. Whether the first bill shows $33.00 or a fraction of it, and whether the allowance is scaled, is unknown until it posts. The tariff model should treat both as bill-observed rather than derived.

## Power Cost Adjustment

Cobb EMC publishes the rider as a one-page schedule, [PCA effective 2026-01-01](https://www.cobbemc.com/sites/default/files/documents/rates/residential/2026/0126_PCA_Rate_Schedule.pdf). It says the corporation adjusts rates "as necessary to follow fluctuations related to wholesale power purchase costs," that rates referring to the schedule "shall be increased or decreased in an amount per kWh for each kWh of energy sold," and that "the PCA factor will remain in effect until the Board of Directors deems it necessary to change the factor in accordance with changes in wholesale power expenses." The board may also layer on a Correction Factor addressing excess or deficiency against an annually set Target Operating Margin. The rider replaced the older WP-12 Wholesale Power Adjustment clause, which is why older bills print the line as "Wholesale Power Adjustment."

So the PCA is a flat per-kWh factor, not a percentage, and it has no schedule of its own changes: it persists until the board changes it. The rider does not publish the factor's value, and the factor does not appear anywhere else on cobbemc.com that I could find; the [Working for You](https://www.cobbemc.com/working-you) page only claims about $86 million returned to members since 2013 and says the amount "can be zero, a charge, or a credit." **The current factor is unknown until the first bill posts.** The 2025 sample bill prints it at $0.00, which is illustrative rather than current.

The NiteFlex schedule carves out the allowance explicitly: "Super Off-Peak kWh under 400 kWh per billing period are excluded from the Power Cost Adjustment." Read against the rider's "for each kWh of energy sold," the PCA base is billed kWh minus the free Super Off-Peak kWh, capped at 400. At current usage, roughly 210 Super Off-Peak kWh per cycle, the exclusion removes all of them, so the PCA base is total kWh minus Super Off-Peak kWh. That relationship stops holding the moment a cycle crosses 400.

## Sales tax

Georgia treats the sale of electricity to any purchaser for a purpose other than resale as a retail sale, [O.C.G.A. 48-8-2(31)(A)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-48/chapter-8/article-1/part-1/section-48-8-2/), and the same section defines tangible personal property to include electricity. There is no residential exemption for electricity: the [DOR exemption list](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2020-list-sales-and-use-tax-exemptions/download) carries exemptions for energy used in manufacturing and for gas burned to generate electricity for resale, and nothing for household use. An attorney general opinion annotated under 48-8-3 held that electric service sold by a rural electric membership corporation is taxed "on the total charges billed to such subscribers for electric service," which is the answer to the base question: the service charge is part of the base, not an untaxed fixed fee. Statutory "sales price" in 48-8-2(34) points the same way, since it is the total consideration without deduction for the seller's costs.

The rate is the ordinary jurisdiction rate. DOR's [general rate chart effective January 1, 2026](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/general-rate-chart-effective-january-1-2026-through-march-31-2026/download) lists jurisdiction 060, Fulton County outside Atlanta, Hapeville, College Park and East Point, at **7.75%**, composed of the 4% state rate plus MARTA, LOST, the educational tax and the Fulton TSPLOST. Roswell is not carved out, so 7.75% is the rate for this service location. Note the chart is issued quarterly, so the tariff model should not treat the rate as permanent.

Cobb EMC's own schedule says only that "the member shall pay any sales, use, franchise or other tax now or hereafter applicable," and its rate selection tool states that its illustrative bill totals exclude taxes and fees. The bill prints one combined line, "State and Local Taxes," not a state and local split.

The 2025 sample bill supports both the base and the ordering. One sample: $29.00 service charge, $93.25 energy, $0.00 wholesale power adjustment, $8.57 tax, $0.18 round up, total $131.00. The tax is 7.00% of the $122.25 of charges above it, and the round up is what lifts $130.82 to a whole dollar. The other sample: $90.28 base, $6.32 tax, $0.40 round up, $97.00 total, and $90.28 x 0.07 = $6.32 to the cent. Two illustrations at 7.00% do not establish the rate for any real jurisdiction, and neither matches Cobb County's 6% at the time, so read them as arithmetic demonstrations only. What they do establish is that tax is computed on service charge plus energy plus PCA, and that Operation Round Up comes last.

## Operation Round Up

The account is enrolled, which is the default: "all Cobb EMC members are automatically enrolled in Operation Round Up and will remain in the program until they request to be removed from it." Every month the bill is rounded up to the next dollar, at most 99 cents, at most $11.88 a year, with the difference going to the Cobb EMC Foundation. The sample bill arithmetic above shows it applied to the post-tax total, so it is the final line and never itself taxed. January statements carry the annual contribution total in the Message Center for tax purposes.

For the integration this is a per-cycle amount under a dollar that depends on every other line, including the PCA nobody knows in advance. It cannot be predicted mid-cycle and is worth carrying only in the true-up, if at all.

## Franchise fee and other municipal charges

Georgia municipalities may condition a street franchise on an EMC's payment of a franchise fee, and Roswell collects electric franchise revenue: its [FY2026 approved budget](http://roswellgov.com/wp-content/uploads/City-of-Roswell-Approved-Budget-FY2026.pdf) budgets $4,905,600 of Electric Franchise Taxes in the general fund. The budget does not break that down by provider, and I found no Roswell ordinance text online stating a rate for electric membership corporations; the city's Chapter 24, Utilities and Services, covers city water, sewer and stormwater only, and Chapter 10 would not load. **Whether Cobb EMC pays Roswell a franchise fee, and at what rate, is not established from a primary source here.**

What is established is that no published Cobb EMC bill, sample or explanatory, shows a franchise fee as a member line item. Cobb EMC recovers such costs through rates, and its Tax Adjustment clause reserves the right to bill any franchise tax "imposed on the Corporation because of such service" without saying it does. Treat a franchise line as possible but unobserved, and let the first bill settle it. There is no city of Roswell electric utility charge of any kind: Roswell's own utility services are water and sanitation.

## Everything else Cobb EMC can itemize

The Service Rules and Regulations, board approved and revised 2022-02-15, are the source for the one-off charges. The service establishment fee is $30.00 and applies at connect and at transfer, so it is a plausible line on this account's first bill given the 2026-08-28 connect date. A service security deposit may be required and is refundable, ordinarily capped at two months of estimated billing. Past due balances draw a late payment charge of $10.00 or 1.5%, whichever is greater, with payment due 20 days after the billing date. A delinquent reconnect is $40.00, or $300.00 when a crew must work somewhere other than the meter. A returned check is $25.00. Refusing an AMI meter costs $20.00 a month, which is moot here since the meter is AMI and reports hourly.

Capital credits are the pleasant surprise. Cobb EMC retires patronage capital annually, and current members receive it as a bill credit on the July or August bill under the line "Capital Credit Cash Back," or by check if the amount is $500 or more. The 2026 distribution is $10.8 million for 2025 members. It is sized by the prior year's purchases, announced by the board, and appears in exactly one cycle a year. A cost statistic that must match the bill total will be wrong by that credit for one cycle unless the true-up carries it.

Outdoor lighting is billed per fixture from a separate schedule and does not apply here. Even Bill accounts hide the PCA inside the levelized amount rather than printing it; irrelevant on NiteFlex, but it explains why some Cobb EMC bills lack the line entirely.

## What this means for the design

The partition the true-up consumes comes out like this. Computable from the schedule at any time: the three energy period charges, the tier and its allowance, the service charge, the minimum charge, and the sales tax rate. Computable only when the bill posts: the PCA factor, the proration of a short or long cycle, the exact taxable base and its rounding, the round up, the once-off fees, the annual capital credit, and whether a franchise line exists at all.

The consequence is that a mid-cycle cost estimate is energy plus service charge plus 7.75%, with the PCA as a known unknown, and a finalized cycle needs at minimum the PCA factor and the read dates lifted off the bill. The bill is also the only place any of this is observable: no SmartHub endpoint found so far returns cost or itemization, and with zero bills issued the itemization for this account is unverified. The first posted bill is worth reading line by line and folding back into this file.
